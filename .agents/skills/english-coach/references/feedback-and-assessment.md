# Feedback and assessment

## Classify feedback accurately

Use these labels when they improve clarity:

- `錯誤`: Grammar, word choice, or meaning is incorrect.
- `不自然`: Understandable, but a native speaker would normally phrase it differently in this context.
- `語氣調整`: Correct wording needs a different register or level of directness.
- `可選說法`: The original is already correct; the alternative is optional.

Do not exaggerate minor stylistic preferences. Praise a natural sentence without manufacturing a correction.

## Prioritize teaching value

Prioritize, in order:

1. Meaning or task failure
2. Recurring grammar patterns
3. Collocation and idiomatic word choice
4. Register and pragmatics
5. Minor style or punctuation

Prefer explanations that transfer to future situations. Use short contrastive examples when they are more useful than grammatical terminology.

## Judge proficiency conservatively

Assess dimensions separately:

- grammatical control
- active vocabulary range
- collocation and naturalness
- coherence and response complexity
- register and pragmatic appropriateness
- reading comprehension, when tested
- writing task completion, when tested

For every formal assessment:

- Identify the evidence used and its limitations.
- Give a confidence level of low, medium, or high.
- Treat CEFR levels as provisional ranges unless multiple representative tasks support a narrower conclusion.
- Describe active vocabulary observed in production. Do not convert unique words from a few sessions into a total vocabulary-size claim.
- Estimate reading only from reading tasks, writing only from writing tasks, and listening only from audio-based tasks.
- Give a TOEIC-related range only after relevant, sufficiently broad, preferably timed tasks. State whether it refers to Reading, Listening, Speaking, or Writing; never infer unmeasured sections.

## Update learning evidence

- Count a repeated occurrence of the same issue at most once per session for pattern-frequency purposes.
- Keep a one-off issue as session evidence; promote it to a recurring weakness only after independent recurrence.
- Mark improvement only when later independent production supports it.
- Avoid treating short answers as proof that the learner lacks more advanced vocabulary or grammar.
